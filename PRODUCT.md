# Product

## Register

product

## Users

The primary users are the author and invited readers of Morningstar: The Crimson Wolf. They read long-form fiction on desktop and mobile devices, often in focused sessions where comfort, continuity, and low-friction navigation matter more than social features.

Readers need to move between Parts and chapters, resume the exact section they last read, adjust typography for their device and eyesight, and consult full lore about characters and regions without leaving the reading flow.

## Product Purpose

Morningstar is a web-based novel reader for a completed long-form manuscript. It turns the existing source documents into a responsive reading experience with Part and chapter navigation, per-chapter progress, Last Read, manual bookmarks, reader settings, and an encyclopedia for characters and regions.

Success means the app feels like a modern private book, not a blog, wiki, game menu, or social reading feed. The prose remains the center of attention; controls appear when needed and recede when the reader is immersed.

The first version is local-first: reader settings, progress, Last Read, and bookmarks are stored in the current browser. Cross-device account sync is not part of the initial product.

## Brand Personality

Restrained, literary, atmospheric.

The product voice is mature, calm, and intimate. It should carry the weight of dark fantasy and adult emotional stakes without becoming theatrical, ornamental, or noisy. The interface should feel controlled and precise, with mood expressed through typography, spacing, reading rhythm, and a rare crimson accent.

## Anti-references

This product must not feel like:

- A Wattpad-like social reader with bright badges, feeds, reactions, clutter, or adolescent fanfiction energy.
- A game HUD or RPG codex with heavy panels, fantasy ornament, glowing effects, or inventory-screen density.
- A plain blog where chapters read like posts instead of a book.
- A generic wiki where lore competes with the act of reading.
- A parchment-themed fantasy site with decorative borders, scroll textures, faux medieval styling, or overused cream-and-brown nostalgia.
- A SaaS dashboard with card-heavy layouts, hero metrics, promotional sections, or marketing copy.

## Design Principles

1. Reader first. The prose owns the screen; navigation and tools should feel discoverable but quiet.

2. Book comfort across screens. Desktop and mobile layouts must preserve line length, paragraph rhythm, legible controls, and stable progress even when text size changes.

3. Lore at hand, never in the way. Encyclopedia access should be fast and contextual, but it should not interrupt reading unless the reader asks for it.

4. Open lore access. Character and region facts are available from the start in their encyclopedia section; this is a novel companion, not a quest-locked codex.

5. Progress without friction. Last Read, chapter progress, and bookmarks should work automatically in the browser without requiring accounts in version 1.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

The baseline is WCAG AA. The app must support readable color contrast, keyboard navigation, visible focus states, reduced motion, scalable text, touch-friendly controls, and layouts that do not overlap or break on mobile.

Color must never be the only way to convey state. Reading controls should remain understandable with icons, labels, and tooltips where needed. Motion should be limited to state feedback and drawer transitions, with reduced-motion alternatives.

The source material includes mature themes and mature romantic content. The interface should handle this with respect and restraint. If the app is deployed publicly rather than privately, it should include an appropriate content advisory and a rights/publication review before launch.
